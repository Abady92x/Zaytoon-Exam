📚 Chapter 4-1: Hazard Classification & Process Safety

🎯 Multiple Choice Questions

Q1: The following is correct about hazardous area classification:
(a) Division 1 is more dangerous than Division 2
(b) Class III is more dangerous than Class I & II
(c) Group D is more dangerous than Group A
(d) Dusts and fibers are classified Class I
Answer: (a) Division 1 is more dangerous than Division 2. Division 1 represents hazardous conditions that are more likely to exist during normal operation, making it more dangerous.
Q2: The following is correct about the use of industrial trucks in hazardous areas:
(a) LP and LPS have similar safety features
(b) DY has less safety features than D
(c) EX is safer than ES
(d) EE is the safest industrial truck
Answer: (d) EE is the safest industrial truck. EE trucks have electric systems completely enclosed and are designed for the most hazardous environments.
Q3: The following industrial trucks can be used in a hazardous area of Class I, Group B, Division 1:
(a) EX only
(b) DY, EE & EX
(c) DS, DY, ES
(d) None of the above
Answer: (b) DY, EE & EX. These trucks have the necessary safety features to operate in Class I, Group B, Division 1 hazardous locations.
Q4: The following unit has, most probably, a Class II hazardous location:
(a) Gasoline storage tank
(b) Aluminum powder storage area
(c) Textile plant
(d) Gas storage tanks
Answer: (b) Aluminum powder storage area. Class II involves combustible dust hazards, of which aluminum powder is a classic example.
Q5: The following is NOT true about process knowledge principles:
(a) Process knowledge should remain useful
(b) Support efforts to properly manage change
(c) Protect knowledge from being accessed by workers
(d) Protect knowledge from inadvertent loss
Answer: (c) Protect knowledge from being accessed by workers. Process knowledge SHOULD be accessible to workers for safety and operational effectiveness.
Q6: The following is NOT true about process knowledge principles:
(a) Protect and update process knowledge
(b) Use process knowledge
(c) Maintain an undependable practice
(d) Catalog process knowledge in a manner that facilitates retrieval
Answer: (c) Maintain an undependable practice. This is incorrect; we should maintain DEPENDABLE practices based on process knowledge.
Q7: The following is NOT true about the application of HIRA:
(a) HIRA can't be applied during the operation phase of the lifecycle
(b) HIRA is best applied earlier in the lifecycle
(c) The earlier HIRA is applied in the lifecycle, the more cost effective it is
(d) There are common activities in both HIRA and risk assessment
Answer: (a) HIRA can't be applied during the operation phase - This is FALSE. HIRA CAN be applied during operations for continuous improvement and hazard reassessment.
Q8: The following is a quantitative HIRA technique:
(a) HAZOP
(b) What-If
(c) FTA
(d) FMEA
Answer: (c) FTA (Fault Tree Analysis). FTA is a quantitative technique that uses mathematical/logical operators to calculate failure probabilities.

🔍 Q3: Why Perform Only Necessary Level of Analysis in HIRA?
  • Insufficient analysis: May lead to poor decisions and missed hazards
  • Excessive analysis: Wastes valuable resources and delays decision-making
Balance is critical - conduct enough analysis to be thorough but not so much that resources are wasted on diminishing returns.
✅ Q4: True/False Statements (Corrected)
1. Process knowledge management means creation and organizing information.
Correction: Process knowledge management includes creating, organizing, maintaining, and providing information - not just creation and organization.
2. Poor process knowledge negatively affects process safety competency.
✓ TRUE - Poor process knowledge directly undermines the ability to operate safely.
3. D, G & LP trucks are not allowed to enter any hazardous area.
Correction: D, G, and LP trucks are prohibited in SOME hazardous areas (e.g., Class I Division 1), but may be allowed in other hazardous areas (e.g., Class I Division 2) if properly rated.
4. Maintaining dependable practice means all RBPSM activities should be based on process knowledge.
✓ TRUE - Dependable practice is built on solid, current process knowledge.
5. What-If analysis and Safety Reviews are simple techniques requiring only one person.
Correction: What-If analysis and Safety Reviews are group-based techniques that require multiple participants for diverse perspectives.
6. Formal Safety Reviews are most suitable for small changes to existing processes.
Correction: Formal Safety Reviews are used for new processes and substantial changes, while informal reviews are used for small changes.
📝 Q5: Key Definitions

Process Safety Information includes three aspects:

  1. Physical properties
  2. Chemical properties
  3. Toxicological data

Three Important Diagrams:

  • Block Flow Diagram: Shows major equipment or units
  • Process Flow Diagram: Shows pressure, temperature, composition, flow rate, etc.
  • Piping & Instrumentation Diagram (P&ID): Shows details about piping, vessels, and instrumentation

Key Acronyms:

  • HIRA: Hazard Identification & Risk Analysis
  • Formal Safety Reviews: Used for new processes or substantial changes
🛡️ Chapter 4-2: Layers of Protection Analysis (LOPA)

🎯 Multiple Choice Questions

Q9: The following is true about LOPA:
(a) It uses order-of-magnitude categories of probability
(b) It identifies potential scenarios of concern by itself
(c) LOPA does not need the results of other analysis methods to build on
(d) LOPA is a qualitative method
Answer: (a) It uses order-of-magnitude categories of probability. LOPA employs probability categories (not precise calculations) to assess risk.
Q10: LOPA can be a useful tool to assess and manage risk during:
(a) The early design phase of a process
(b) The installation phase of a process
(c) The operation phase of a process
(d) Any phase in the lifecycle of a process
Answer: (d) Any phase in the lifecycle of a process. LOPA is flexible and can be applied throughout the entire lifecycle.
Q11: A device failure, system failure, external event, or wrong action that begins a sequence of events:
(a) Common cause failure
(b) Initiating event
(c) Enabling condition
(d) Conditional modifier
Answer: (b) Initiating event. This is the starting point of a hazardous scenario.
Q12: The following is true about layers of protection:
(a) Some of them are preventive and others are mitigative
(b) All of them should be preventive
(c) All of them should be of the active type
(d) All of them should be of the passive type
Answer: (a) Some of them are preventive and others are mitigative. A robust protection system includes both prevention and mitigation layers.
Q13: The most effective layer of protection is:
(a) Relief devices
(b) Critical alarms and human intervention
(c) Process design
(d) Safety instrumented functions
Answer: (c) Process design. Inherent safety through design is the most effective and reliable layer of protection.
Q14: Dikes are:
(a) Enabling conditions
(b) Passive IPLs
(c) Active IPLs
(d) Preventive IPLs
Answer: (b) Passive IPLs. Dikes are physical structures that provide passive, mitigative protection without requiring activation.
✅ LOPA True/False Statements
7. Bow-tie analysis can be used as input to LOPA.
✓ TRUE - Bow-tie diagrams identify scenarios that can be analyzed using LOPA.
8. All safeguards are independent protection layers.
✗ FALSE - All IPLs are safeguards, but not every safeguard meets the requirements of being an IPL (independence, effectiveness, etc.).
9. Independent protection layers should be powered by the same power source.
✗ FALSE - Independent protection layers should have independent power or utility sources to maintain their independence.
10. A common cause failure is failure of more than one device due to the same cause.
✓ TRUE - Common cause failure affects multiple systems simultaneously.
11. When one protection layer depends on another, the layers are independent.
✗ FALSE - When performance of one layer depends on another, they are NOT independent.
📚 LOPA Key Concepts

Methods to Determine Initiating Event Frequency:

  • Site specific: Data from this specific facility
  • Predicted: Calculated based on models
  • Generic: Industry standard data
  • Expert judgement: Professional assessment

Key Definitions:

  • Enabling Condition: Operating conditions necessary for an initiating cause to propagate into a hazardous event
  • Conditional Modifier: The probability of hazardous atmosphere, ignition, explosion, personnel presence, injury or fatality
🔬 LOPA Case Study: Separator 50 Overpressure Analysis

Scenario Description:

Consequence: Separator 50 overpressure resulting in separator rupture and possible multiple fatalities

Initiating Event: Loop failure of PIC-50 BPCS

Component Value Description
Initiating Event Frequency 0.1 per year Failure of PIC-50 BPCS pressure control loop
Enabling Condition 1.0 (None) No enabling condition identified
Personnel Probability 0.5 Probability of personnel in affected area
Fatal Injury Probability 0.1 Probability of fatal injuries given exposure
``` Unmitigated Consequence Frequency: f = 0.1 × 1 × (0.5 × 0.1) = 0.005 per year
```

Independent Protection Layers (IPLs):

IPL Description Type PFD
Inherently safer design Prevention/Design 0.01
Safety Instrumented Function (PAHH & PAH) Detection & Response 0.01
Pressure Safety Valve (PSV-50) Relief/Mitigation 0.01
``` Total PFD for all IPLs: ∏PFD = 0.01 × 0.01 × 0.01 = 0.000001 = 1 × 10⁻⁶
```
``` Mitigated Consequence Frequency: f = 0.1 × 0.000001 × 1 × (0.5 × 0.1) f = 0.1 × 0.000001 × 0.05 f = 5 × 10⁻⁹ per year = 0.000000005
```

✅ Risk Assessment Result:

Risk Tolerance Level: Category 5 with frequency < 10⁻⁶ per year

Conclusion: The mitigated consequence frequency (5 × 10⁻⁹) is well below the acceptable threshold of 10⁻⁶

Action Required: ✓ No additional risk reduction actions required. Existing IPLs are adequate provided they are properly maintained and tested.

🏭 Chapter 5: Operating Procedures & Management of Change

🎯 Multiple Choice Questions

Q1: The following is true about Operating Procedures (OPs):
(a) Designed for nonroutine tasks
(b) Applicable mainly to tasks requiring permits
(c) Written for use across a facility or the entire organization
(d) Intended to support normal operation within safe operating limits
Answer: (d) Intended to support normal operation within safe operating limits. OPs provide standardized procedures for routine operations.
Q2: The following task requires OPs:
(a) Nonroutine hot works
(b) Startup and shutdown activities
(c) Confined space entry
(d) Nonroutine Inspection
Answer: (b) Startup and shutdown activities. These are critical operational tasks that require formal procedures.
Q3: The following participate in the preparation of OPs:
(a) Engineers
(b) Frontline managers
(c) Operators
(d) All of the above
Answer: (d) All of the above. OP development is multidisciplinary and requires input from all stakeholders.
Q4: "In hand" operating procedures should be used in case of:
(a) Critical tasks
(b) Routine tasks
(c) Manual tasks
(d) Assembly tasks
Answer: (a) Critical tasks. Critical tasks require immediate reference access to procedures during execution.
Q5: The following event requires a management-of-change policy:
(a) Replacing an old vent valve with a new one of the same design
(b) Replacement of a machine with another using different technology
(c) Replacement-in-kind
(d) Confined space entry
Answer: (b) Replacement of a machine with another using different technology. This represents a substantive change requiring MOC review.
Q6: The program associated with efforts to ensure a process is ready for start-up/restart is named:
(a) Management of change
(b) Safety permits
(c) Conduct of operation
(d) Operational readiness
Answer: (d) Operational readiness. This program ensures all systems are prepared and personnel are ready before startup.
✅ Operating Procedures True/False
1. OPs provide instructions for troubleshooting when the system does not respond as expected.
✓ TRUE - OPs typically include troubleshooting sections for abnormal conditions.
2. OPs cannot be used in training since they can't develop operators' performance and skills.
✗ FALSE - OPs CAN be used in training to develop operators' performance and skills.
3. OPs should be designed in the form of outlines.
✗ FALSE - OPs may be prepared in multiple formats including paragraphs, outlines, multicolumn layouts, flowcharts, or checklists depending on the task complexity and user needs.
4. Conduct of operation ensures that every task is performed deliberately and correctly.
✓ TRUE - Conduct of operation establishes the framework for proper task execution.
⚙️ Management of Change (MOC)
Management of Change is a systematic process to identify, evaluate, and implement changes to equipment, materials, procedures, or processes in a way that maintains safety and operability.

Changes Requiring MOC Review:

  • Technology changes (different machines or equipment types)
  • Procedural changes to operations or maintenance
  • Equipment modifications or upgrades
  • Process parameter changes
  • Staffing or organizational changes
  • Regulatory or standards updates

Changes NOT Requiring MOC (Replacement-in-Kind):

  • Direct replacement with identical or equivalent equipment
  • Same specifications and performance characteristics
  • No change in function or operating parameters
📋 Operating Procedures: Best Practices

Format Options for OPs:

  • Paragraphs: Narrative descriptions (verbal procedures)
  • Outlines: Hierarchical structure with main steps and sub-steps
  • Multicolumn layouts: Side-by-side information and actions
  • Flowcharts: Visual representation of decision paths
  • Checklists: Simple tick-off format for step-by-step tasks

OP Content Typically Includes:

  • Purpose and scope of the procedure
  • Normal operating steps with expected conditions
  • Abnormal operation procedures
  • Troubleshooting and problem resolution
  • Emergency shutdown procedures
  • Safety precautions and hazard warnings

Key Programs:

  • Conduct of Operation: Ensures deliberate, correct task execution
  • Operational Readiness: Verifies process readiness before startup/restart
  • Management of Change: Controls and documents changes to the process
